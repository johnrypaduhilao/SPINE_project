# Policy Breakdown: "Wipe the Stovetop Clean"

---

## Pre-Condition Policies

**Policy 1: Verify Stovetop is Cool Before Contact**
- **What it does:** Checks surface temperature before any physical contact or placing materials on the stovetop.
- **Why:** A hot burner causes burns to the robot's end effector, damages cleaning materials, and creates fire/steam hazards. "Clean" implies a safe, completed task — not one that causes secondary damage.

**Policy 2: Confirm Burners are Off**
- **What it does:** Verifies all burner controls are in the OFF position before proceeding.
- **Why:** Wiping an active burner is a direct fire and injury hazard. This is a hard safety gate, not optional.

**Policy 3: Clear Loose Items from Surface**
- **What it does:** Removes pots, pans, grates, or other objects from the stovetop before wiping.
- **Why:** Wiping around or under objects leaves the surface incompletely clean and risks knocking items onto the floor or damaging them.

---

## Cleaning Method Policies

**Policy 4: Select Appropriate Cleaning Agent**
- **What it does:** Chooses a cleaner compatible with the stovetop type (glass-ceramic, gas, electric coil) — avoiding abrasives on glass surfaces, avoiding excess liquid near electrical components.
- **Why:** The wrong cleaner can permanently damage the surface, which contradicts the intent of cleaning it. "Clean" should not mean "damaged."

**Policy 5: Use Appropriate Wiping Tool**
- **What it does:** Selects a non-scratch cloth or sponge appropriate to the surface material.
- **Why:** An abrasive pad on a glass cooktop causes scratches. The tool must match the surface to fulfill the intent without harm.

**Policy 6: Apply Cleaner Before Wiping, Not Directly to Heating Elements**
- **What it does:** Applies cleaning solution to the cloth/sponge rather than spraying directly onto burners, knobs, or electrical components.
- **Why:** Liquid intrusion into burner assemblies or control knobs creates electrical hazards and corrosion.

**Policy 7: Wipe in a Systematic Pattern Covering the Full Surface**
- **What it does:** Executes a defined coverage path (e.g., back-to-front, overlapping strokes) across the entire stovetop area.
- **Why:** "Clean" means the whole surface, not just visible spots. A systematic pattern ensures no area is missed.

**Policy 8: Apply Appropriate Pressure — Not Excessive**
- **What it does:** Regulates force applied during wiping to be sufficient for soil removal but not enough to damage knobs, grates, or surface coatings.
- **Why:** Excessive force can break components; insufficient force leaves residue. Both fail the intent.

---

## Completeness & Quality Policies

**Policy 9: Perform a Visual Inspection Pass After Wiping**
- **What it does:** After the initial wipe, scans the surface for remaining visible residue, grease, or streaks and re-wipes those areas.
- **Why:** "Clean" is the success criterion. A single pass may not be sufficient; the policy enforces the outcome, not just the action.

**Policy 10: Remove Cleaning Residue / Rinse Pass**
- **What it does:** Follows up with a damp clean cloth to remove any cleaning agent residue from the surface.
- **Why:** Leaving chemical residue on a stovetop is a health hazard (residue can vaporize when next heated) and the surface is not truly clean.

**Policy 11: Dry the Surface**
- **What it does:** Wipes the surface dry with a clean dry cloth after the rinse pass.
- **Why:** A wet stovetop near electrical components is a hazard, and water spots on glass cooktops mean the task is visually incomplete.

---

## Post-Condition Policies

**Policy 12: Return Grates and Removable Components**
- **What it does:** Replaces any grates, burner caps, or covers that were removed, in their correct positions.
- **Why:** The stovetop should be left in a functional, ready-to-use state. Leaving components off is an incomplete task.

**Policy 13: Dispose of or Store Cleaning Materials Appropriately**
- **What it does:** Places used cloths in the laundry/waste and returns cleaning agents to storage.
- **Why:** Leaving soiled cloths or open chemicals on the counter creates secondary mess and hazards — the broader environment should not be degraded by the task.

**Policy 14: Log Task Completion with Surface State**
- **What it does:** Records that the task was completed, noting any anomalies (e.g., stain that could not be removed, damaged component observed).
- **Why:** Provides accountability and flags issues that may require human follow-up, ensuring the operator's intent is fully communicated even when perfect completion isn't possible.
